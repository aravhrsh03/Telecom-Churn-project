## 1. First-pass review — "does anything here look wrong?"

Prompt used (deliberately not leading toward an answer):

> Here's predict.py from a churn-prediction FastAPI service. Does anything
> here look wrong or inconsistent?

Response (informal review):

> `predict.py` loads `logistic_churn.pkl` and reads `model.feature_names_in_`
> to get the exact list of columns the model expects — that part is solid,
> it can't drift from whatever the saved model actually needs.
>
> The problem is in `preprocess_input()`. It builds a dict initialized to
> `0` for every one of those columns, then only ever overwrites four of
> them: `tenure`, `monthly_charges`, `service_count`, and one
> `contract_type_*` dummy. Every other column the model was trained on —
> `total_charges`, `high_charge_flag`, `is_long_term_customer`,
> `has_streaming_bundle`, `auto_pay_flag`, the `internet_service_*` dummies,
> and more — is silently left at 0 for every single request, no matter what
> the real customer looks like. The model is fine; the function feeding it
> data is quietly starving it of most of its own inputs.

## 2. Targeted audit question — train.py vs predict.py

Prompt used:

> Compare train.py's feature columns against what predict.py actually
> builds for the same features. Which ones does predict.py get from real
> input, and which ones does it fabricate?

Response:

> `train.py` produces 38 feature columns from `customer_ml_features.csv` —
> 13 numeric/derived columns plus one-hot dummies across 12 categorical
> columns (gender, internet_service, contract, payment_method,
> tenure_bucket, and six Yes/No/"No internet service" service columns).
>
> `predict.py`'s API surface (`ChurnPredictionRequest`) only ever collects
> 4 raw inputs: tenure, monthly_charges, contract_type, service_count. That
> means at best 4 of 38 columns can ever be real. Two more —
> `is_long_term_customer` (a pure function of tenure) and `high_charge_flag`
> (tenure vs. a fixed threshold) — *could* be derived from what's already
> being collected, but weren't. Everything else genuinely has no source in
> the current request shape: there's no way to know a hypothetical
> customer's `internet_service` or `payment_method` from four numbers.
>
> This points to two different fixes with two different sizes:
>  1. **Small, free fix**: compute `is_long_term_customer` and
>     `high_charge_flag` from what's already collected, instead of leaving
>     them at 0.
>  2. **Real fix**: for an actual existing customer, don't guess at all —
>     look up their true engineered feature row from `customer_ml_features`
>     by `customer_id` and score that. Reserve the 4-field "hypothetical"
>     path for genuinely hypothetical scenarios (which is exactly the
>     "what-if" case Lab AI2 tests), and be explicit in the response about
>     which fields were assumed.

This is exactly what `ml/predict.py` does now — see `predict_from_customer_id()`
and `predict_hypothetical()`.

## 4. Plain-English summary (for a non-technical write-up)

The churn-prediction tool was supposed to look at a customer's tenure, bill
size, contract type, and how many services they use, and estimate how
likely they are to cancel. In practice, it was only ever really looking at
tenure, bill size, and contract type — for every other signal the model
was trained on, it was quietly assuming the answer was "no" or "average,"
instead of checking. The prediction still ran and looked reasonable, so
nobody would have noticed just from using it. The fix makes the tool
actually use a customer's real data when it has a customer to look up, and
clearly labels which assumptions it's making on the (much rarer)
what-if path, where by definition there's no real customer to check yet.
